████████░░░░░░░░░░░░ 40%

Hofstadter keeps circling back to the idea of a strange loop, a hierarchy that,
followed far enough, lands back where it started. I still need to work out
whether that is the same idea as a fixed point (A value that a function maps to
itself: f(x) = x.), or just a cousin of it.

A strange loop arises when, by moving only upwards or downwards through the
levels of a hierarchical system, we unexpectedly find ourselves right back where
we started.
                                                  ── Gödel, Escher, Bach, p. 152
Worth rereading before the chapter on Gödel's theorem.
